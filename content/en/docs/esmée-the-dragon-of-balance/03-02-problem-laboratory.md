# 🐉⚖️ ESMÉE. ✯ Problem Laboratory | The Dragon of Balance

![_The Dragon of Balance. 🐉⚖️ ESMÉE. Problem Laboratory. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 09.10.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/esmée-the-dragon-of-balance-03-02-problem-laboratory.webp)

> **The answer is not lying on the table. It has to be built.**

The second chamber is different.

Not because it belongs to another world, but because the same underground system reveals itself from another angle.

Here, the space is filled with problems.

There are tables covered with diagrams, mechanisms, fragments of unfinished constructions, geometric forms, notes, symbols, and elements whose relationships are not immediately clear. Nothing offers a ready-made answer. Every part seems to belong to something larger, but the path between the parts has yet to be discovered.

This is the **Problem Laboratory**.

And its purpose is not to find an answer as quickly as possible.

It is to remain inside the process of solving.

Some problems look impossible precisely because their structure cannot yet be seen. The first step is therefore not always to calculate, explain, or conclude. Sometimes it is simply to stay with the problem long enough for its internal connections to begin appearing.

ESMÉE enters this chamber with her tools.

She does not know the answer in advance.

She examines the mechanisms. She moves one element. Then another. She changes the angle. Tests a possibility. Rejects it. Returns to an earlier point. Connects fragments that seemed unrelated.

The work continues.

And this is where the word **solving** becomes important.

It is not a single act — not the moment when a solution suddenly appears.

It is a process of constructing the path toward the answer.

A problem becomes different once the path through it begins to exist.

The amethyst light in this chamber marks transformation. What first appears as confusion gradually becomes structure. What seemed disconnected begins to interact. What seemed too complicated begins to reveal a possible sequence.

**Complexity does not disappear. It becomes navigable.**

This is how movement takes place within this chamber — through attempts, connections, experiments, reversals, and discoveries.

The answer is not lying on the table.

It is being built.

And perhaps this is one of the quiet lessons of the year: some things cannot be solved by looking for the correct answer. They can only be solved by remaining present long enough to construct the way toward it.

The second chamber of ESMÉE's laboratory is therefore not a place of answers.

It is a place of **solving**.

# Ambitions | The Mindset of Achievement

![_Ambitions | The Mindset of Achievement. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 05.11.2025_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/essay-the-mindset-of-achievement-001-ambitions.webp)

When I hear the word _ambition_, the first thing that comes to mind is the pursuit of goals. To me, ambition is an inner driving force that propels us forward. I consider myself an ambitious person because I believe this energy is essential for growth. Ambition continually encourages us to develop, learn, and become better versions of ourselves.

As a child, my ambitions were relatively simple. Like many children, I wanted to achieve things that would make my parents proud. Today, my ambitions are more focused on personal development — for example, learning something new every day. This is an achievable goal. At the same time, there are ambitions beyond the limits of possibility, such as becoming taller or turning back the clock.

Ambition is a double-edged sword. It can inspire and empower us, but it can also exhaust us when we become excessively demanding of ourselves or others. The real danger begins when we resort to morally questionable or destructive methods to achieve what we want.

I often think about the words attributed to Thomas Carlyle: _"I have a great ambition — to die of exhaustion rather than boredom."_ I can certainly relate to this sentiment. I tend to take on a lot, juggling several tasks at once. For me, a life filled with purpose and activity, even when demanding, is far more valuable than one spent in idleness. Yet ambition should not become an excuse for self-destruction.

There is a popular saying often attributed to Thomas Jefferson: _"Nothing can stop a person with the right mental attitude from achieving their goal; nothing on earth can help a person with the wrong mental attitude."_ I agree with the first part, but I cannot accept the second. History has shown us that people driven by destructive ambitions — such as Hitler or Putin — can achieve their horrific objectives, unleashing wars and genocides. This is a chilling reminder that the absence of a moral compass does not necessarily lead to failure, at least not initially.

> 🖋️ _"Ambition without morality is a flame without light."_

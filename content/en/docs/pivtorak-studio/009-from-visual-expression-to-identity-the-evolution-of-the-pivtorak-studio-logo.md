# From Visual Expression to Identity: The Evolution of the Pivtorak.Studio Logo | The Formula of Transformation

![_From Visual Expression to Identity: The Evolution of the Pivtorak.Studio Logo | The Formula of Transformation. AP | Pivtorak.Studio 14.10.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/pivtorak-studio-009-from-visual-expression-to-identity-the-evolution-of-the-pivtorak-studio-logo.webp)

A logo is not merely a graphic mark. It is an identification tool that must function within the brand's environment. In July 2026, Pivtorak.Studio developed a visual concept based on the cybernetic Black Box model: a black square, a turquoise Input beam, and a coral Output beam. It communicated the methodology of transformation but proved too visually active for the website's diverse content. During the website redesign on October 14, 2026, a new mark emerged — restrained, geometric, and monochromatic. This was not a rejection of the original idea, but a separation of functions: the methodology gained its own visual language, while the brand acquired an independent identity mark.

## 1. The Original Identity: Visualizing a Cybernetic System

The original Pivtorak.Studio logo was more than a graphic composition. It represented a visual model of the methodology built around the cybernetic principle of the Black Box.

![_From Visual Expression to Identity: The Evolution of the Pivtorak.Studio Logo | The Formula of Transformation. AP | Pivtorak.Studio 14.10.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/pivtorak-studio-009-from-visual-expression-to-identity-the-evolution-of-the-pivtorak-studio-logo-01.webp)

Its components had distinct visual and symbolic functions:

- **The Black Square** — the central geometric element, providing a defined visual framework for the composition.  
- **The Two Diagonal Lines** — turquoise and coral neon lines expressing direction, movement, and a sense of purposeful progression.  
- **P.S.** — the studio's signature, integrated into the lower-right area of the composition.  

The diagonals were not direct representations of the Input and Output signals of the Black Box model. Their meaning belonged to a different symbolic level: the visual expression of a way of thinking, choosing directions, and making decisions.

### The Diagonal as a Vector of Thought and Decision-Making

The two diagonal lines in the original logo created a sense of direction, movement, and intentional orientation.

Rather than illustrating a specific technical process, they could be interpreted as a visual expression of a mindset in which the direction of development matters as much as the initial conditions.

In this context, the diagonals can be understood through three interconnected principles:

- **Direction** — the ability to identify a vector of development and define a course of action.  
- **Movement** — the transition from an existing state towards a new condition or intended result.  
- **Decision** — the conscious choice of direction and the commitment to follow it.  

Together, these elements formed a visual metaphor for purposeful thinking and transformation.

### Black Box: A Separate Visualization of the Methodology

The Black Box model has its own independent graphic representation. In this composition, the black square represents the system itself, while the signals surrounding it illustrate its interaction with the external environment.

![_From Visual Expression to Identity: The Evolution of the Pivtorak.Studio Logo | The Formula of Transformation. AP | Pivtorak.Studio 14.10.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/pivtorak-studio-009-from-visual-expression-to-identity-the-evolution-of-the-pivtorak-studio-logo-02.webp)

Its components are:

- **The Black Square** — the system itself. A complex mechanism whose internal processes may remain invisible to the external observer while operating according to clearly defined internal principles.  
- **The Turquoise-Blue Beam** — the input signal (Input). External influences, uncertainty, and a flow of complex, unstructured information entering the system.  
- **The Coral-Red Beam** — the output signal (Output). The system's response and the result of transformation: the conversion of complexity into a clearly defined structure.  

Unlike the original logo, this composition explicitly visualizes the relationship between the system and its incoming and outgoing information flows.

The distinction is essential: the diagonal lines belong to the symbolic language of the logo, while the Input and Output beams belong to the visual representation of the Black Box methodology.

This was a coherent graphic interpretation of the concept formulated in the preceding article in the series —  [008 From Identity to Methodology: The Black Box of Pivtorak.Studio](/en/docs/pivtorak-studio/008-from-identity-to-methodology-the-black-box-of-pivtorak-studio/).

In effect, the logo attempted to tell the entire story of the brand simultaneously.

It was conceptual, expressive, technological, and a self-contained visual statement. Neon beams, luminous effects, colour contrasts, and diagonal movement created a strong graphic accent.

Against an isolated black background, this worked exceptionally well. However, the independent effectiveness of a graphic mark does not automatically guarantee its compatibility with every environment in which it is used.

And this is where a new design challenge emerged.

## 2. The Visual Conflict: When Identity Competes with Content

Pivtorak.Studio is not a single-page website built around one visual theme. It is a multifaceted environment that brings together research, cultural projects, artworks, digital documentation, tools, and multilingual publications.

Its visual content is fundamentally diverse:

- photographs and artworks;  
- historical images and artefacts;  
- illustrations with different colour systems;  
- research materials;  
- tables, diagrams, and mathematical models;  
- digital tools and documentation.

Within such an environment, the neon logo began competing with the primary content for attention.

A design contradiction emerged: the identity intended to unify diverse content had itself become another dominant visual object.

The problem was not the quality of the original design, but the nature of its interaction with the website's actual environment.

This distinction is fundamental.

The previous logo was not unsuccessful. It accurately represented the stage at which the Black Box concept was being developed and visually communicated. However, as the content architecture expanded, a different approach to identification became necessary.

**An identity should unify diversity, not create additional competition within it.**

## 3. The October 14, 2026 Redesign: A New Design Challenge

During the website redesign on October 14, 2026, a new favicon and a new Pivtorak.Studio logo were created.

This was not an isolated cosmetic update. Both elements were developed within a unified design objective: to establish a recognizable visual identity capable of functioning across different scales and environments.

The new design strategy involved moving away from excessive visual expression towards form, proportion, geometry, and contrast.

Instead of showing how the system works, the logo now simply identifies which system is present.

Its construction:

|Element|Visual Function|
|---|---|
|Black Square|Permanent geometric foundation|
|White P.S.|Primary identification mark|
|Geometric Letterforms|Structure, precision, and order|
|Two Dots|Part of the typographic composition and rhythm|
|Absence of Colour Effects|Neutrality in relation to surrounding content|

The typographic construction is particularly important.

The letter P has a clearly defined vertical stem and a geometric upper section. The S is built from a combination of straight lines and smooth transitions.

Together, they form a compact monogram.

The dots following the letters preserve the written form P.S., rather than turning it into an abstract combination of two initials.

The white mark does not attempt to establish its own colour atmosphere. It works through form, proportion, and contrast.

This is a fundamentally different design strategy.

## 4. From Visualizing Function to Visualizing Identity

The principal difference between the two logos lies not in the number of elements or the colours used.

It lies in the very function of the mark.

The previous logo demonstrated the methodology. The new logo identifies its bearer.

This is a transition from visualizing function to visualizing identity.

|Criterion|Previous Logo|New Logo|
|---|---|---|
|Core Principle|Explanation|Identification|
|Visual Language|Cybernetic Graphics|Geometric Typography|
|Colour|Turquoise, Coral|Black, White|
|Composition|Dynamic Diagonal|Compact Static Composition|
|Visual Activity|High|Low|
|Role on the Website|Standalone Accent|Identifier|
|Context Dependency|Requires a Controlled Environment|More Easily Integrated into Diverse Environments|

This is not a transition from complex design to simple design.

It is a separation of two functions that were previously combined within a single graphic object:

1. The methodology receives its own visual language.  
2. The brand receives its own identification mark.  

The original concept does not lose its value. It can continue to exist as a separate graphic language for presentations, diagrams, covers, and materials dedicated to the Black Box.

The new logo does not cancel the previous idea. It frees that idea from the obligation to serve as the brand's permanent identifier.

## 5. The Principle of Visual Neutrality: Coexistence Instead of Competition

For Pivtorak.Studio, this is perhaps the most important outcome of the redesign.

The new logo does not impose its own aesthetic on the content.

An artwork can remain vivid, restrained, historical, abstract, or photographic. The logo does not require it to conform to its colour palette.

It ensures the continuity of the brand identity regardless of the materials displayed on the page.

This allows us to define three primary functions of the new identity.

**1. Visual Compatibility.**

The mark does not create additional colour conflicts or require other materials to adapt to its own graphic language.

**2. Identity Consistency.**

The logo remains recognizable across different contexts, regardless of the subject matter, stylistic approach, or colour system of the primary content.

**3. Functional Restraint.**

The brand can be present without constantly having to demonstrate itself.

A mature identity does not necessarily need to be the most prominent element on a page. It should be recognizable when noticed and unobtrusive when attention is directed elsewhere.

### Formalizing the Principle of Visual Neutrality

This design logic can be represented through a conceptual model:


$$
V_{\text{total}} = V_{\text{content}} + V_{\text{identity}}
$$


where:

- $V_{\text{total}}$ — the overall visual activity of the page;  
- $V_{\text{content}}$ — the visual activity of the primary content;  
- $V_{\text{identity}}$ — the visual activity of the identification elements.  

For a content-rich environment, the desired principle is:

$$
V_{\text{identity}} \leq V_{\text{content}}
$$

This is neither a universal law of graphic design nor a measurable physical quantity. It is a conceptual rule of visual hierarchy: the identity should not unnecessarily capture attention intended for the content.

Its purpose is to support the coherence of the environment, not to compete with its components.

## 6. One Identity, Multiple Environments

Another important criterion of the new logo is its versatility.

A favicon, website, social media profile, document, and presentation each have different requirements regarding size, contrast, proportions, and the way graphic elements are displayed.

A logo that looks effective only in one specific format cannot fully perform the role of a universal identifier.

The new P.S. mark has a structural advantage: it consists of a small number of large, high-contrast elements.

This provides the potential for use across different digital environments:

- website favicon;  
- primary logo;  
- social media profiles;  
- digital documents;  
- presentations;  
- author publications;  
- future Pivtorak.Studio digital tools.  

The favicon is a particularly revealing test.

At small sizes, fine details, luminous effects, and complex compositions lose their clarity. A geometric mark with strong contrast has better conditions for preserving recognizability.

Therefore, the new logo is not simply a new image. It is a mark capable of unifying the studio's presence across different environments.

## 7. Separating Methodology from Identity: A Systemic Evolution

The change of logo has a deeper connection with the overall development of Pivtorak.Studio.

The previous article [008 From Identity to Methodology: The Black Box of Pivtorak.Studio](/en/docs/pivtorak-studio/008-from-identity-to-methodology-the-black-box-of-pivtorak-studio/) described the transition from an author-led project to an authorial methodology, and from individual publications to an architecture of knowledge.

Now, an analogous process is taking place within the visual identity.

|Level|Previous State|New State|
|---|---|---|
|Methodology|Process Demonstration|Established System|
|Identity|Expressive Graphic Metaphor|Concise Identifier|
|Visual Communication|Emphasis on the Brand Itself|Emphasis on Content|
|Presence|Through Expression|Through Recognition|

This allows us to formalize the new architecture of the visual system.

Let us define:

- $M$ — Methodology;  
- $I$ — Visual Identity;  
- $G_M$ — the graphic language of the methodology;  
- $G_I$ — the graphic mark of the identity.  

Then:


$$
M \longrightarrow G_M
$$



$$
I \longrightarrow G_I
$$


And the overall visual system:


$$
G_{\text{P.S.}} = G_M + G_I
$$


This means that the methodology and the brand no longer need to use the same graphic instrument to perform different functions.

They remain parts of one system, but each receives its own means of visual representation.

**A system does not need to constantly demonstrate its own complexity in order to remain recognizable.**

This is particularly important for a project that aims to develop as an open architecture of knowledge rather than as a collection of publications dependent on the author's continuous presence.

## 8. Design Completion: When Nothing Else Needs to Change

There is another criterion that is difficult to measure but has considerable practical significance.

After the new logo was created, the desire to improve it disappeared.

There was no longer a perceived need to add colour, luminous effects, decorative elements, new graphic effects, or more complex compositions.

This does not mean that the design must remain unchanged forever.

It means that, at the current stage, it meets the established functional requirements and presents no obvious problem that needs to be solved.

This state can be represented through a conceptual formula:

$$
D_{\text{complete}} =
F_{\text{fulfilled}} -
N_{\text{unresolved}}
$$

where:

- $D_{\text{complete}}$ — the state of design completion;  
- $F_{\text{fulfilled}}$ — the fulfilment of functional requirements;  
- $N_{\text{unresolved}}$ — the number of unresolved functional needs.  


This is not a quantitative assessment of design, but a decision-making model.

Completion does not occur when nothing more can be added, but when nothing more needs to be added.

In this context, refusing further complexity becomes not a limitation of creativity, but the result of a precise design decision.

**The new logo does not need further complexity. Its value lies precisely in the fact that it does not attempt to be even more expressive, more technological, or more original.**

## Conclusion. From a Symbol of the System to a Mark of Its Identity

![_From Visual Expression to Identity: The Evolution of the Pivtorak.Studio Logo | The Formula of Transformation. AP | Pivtorak.Studio 14.10.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/pivtorak-studio-009-from-visual-expression-to-identity-the-evolution-of-the-pivtorak-studio-logo-03.webp)

On October 14, 2026, more than a simple replacement of the Pivtorak.Studio logo took place.

Two functions that had previously been combined within a single graphic object were separated.

First, we created a symbol that explained how the system works. Then, we created a mark that no longer needs to explain anything.

This is neither simplification nor a loss of the original idea.

The Black Box cybernetic model remains part of the Pivtorak.Studio methodology. The turquoise Input and coral Output retain their meaning as elements of a separate graphic language. However, they no longer need to be mandatory attributes of every manifestation of the brand.

The new P.S. assumes a different role: to serve as a stable, recognizable, and neutral identifier within a diverse content environment.

This transformation demonstrates an important principle of system design:

**An identity should not compete with the content it represents.**

A strong identity does not need to constantly fight for attention. It only needs to remain recognizable without distracting from what truly matters.

## P.S.

Sometimes the most precise design decision is not to add another element, but to remove, at the right moment, what is no longer necessary.

# Hot Chestnuts | Volunteering

![_Hot Chestnuts | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 18.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-001-hot-chestnuts.webp)

**18 November 2026  | VOL'MIG | Pista Mágica | Loja Social de Esposende | G2**

_While chestnuts are hot, they are easier to peel. And working together makes it quicker._

I came from Kyiv — a city with its own chestnut symbolism — to Portugal, where chestnuts have a rather different meaning. Here, they are not a city symbol but, above all, an edible autumn fruit associated with local cuisine and the tradition of São Martinho.

Kyiv’s chestnuts bring to mind the aesthetics of the city — trees lining its streets and growing in its parks. Portuguese chestnuts evoke the harvest, the fire and the smell of freshly roasted fruit. A familiar image takes on a different meaning: instead of admiring the trees, here you can taste their fruit.

## A Shared Space

Our meeting took place at Centro Comunitário de Vila Chã, a place where different generations come together. Older people spend time here, school-age children also visit, and various activities and shared events are organised. Children perform for the older visitors, who enjoy watching and applaud them. The atmosphere is lively and welcoming, and community life is made up of many simple everyday activities.

![_Hot Chestnuts | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 18.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-001-hot-chestnuts-01.webp)

This time, our task was to peel roasted chestnuts. Wearing thin gloves, we took the hot nuts from a large metal container, removed their shells and placed the peeled chestnuts on a plate.

Two layers had to be removed: the hard outer shell and the thin inner skin, which clings closely to the flesh. The task is not complicated, but it requires attention and speed.

Before roasting, chestnuts are scored so that steam can escape as they heat up and the nuts do not burst. Different methods of heat treatment can be used before peeling — roasting, boiling or steaming. There are also special knives with short, curved blades and serrated chestnut cutters that make scoring the shells easier.

But chestnuts are easiest to peel while they are still hot. Once they cool, the inner skin often becomes more difficult to remove. That is why it is important not to leave the task for too long. When several people work together, an entire batch can be peeled more quickly while the chestnuts are still warm. This is precisely why the task lends itself well to being done together.

## Edible Chestnuts

![_Hot Chestnuts | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 18.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-001-hot-chestnuts-02.webp)

In Portugal, the sweet chestnut — _Castanea sativa_ — has a long history. It was particularly important in the mountainous regions of the north and centre of the country. Before potatoes became widespread, chestnuts made up a significant part of the local diet. They were boiled, roasted, dried for long-term storage, ground into flour and used to make bread and other dishes. It is no coincidence that in some regions the chestnut tree was called _árvore do pão_ — the bread tree.

Unlike most nuts, chestnuts are high in starch and relatively low in fat. They contain dietary fibre, potassium, phosphorus, magnesium and other nutrients. Today, chestnuts remain one of the characteristic flavours of the Portuguese autumn. They are eaten roasted or boiled and added to soups, side dishes and desserts.

Street vendors traditionally call out to passers-by:

> _Quentes e boas!_    
> Hot and delicious!

## The São Martinho Celebration

Chestnuts hold a special place in the celebration of São Martinho, which falls on 11 November. On this day, and around that time, various communities hold _Magusto_ gatherings — traditional get-togethers featuring roasted chestnuts, bonfires and local drinks such as _jeropiga_, _água-pé_ and young wine.

![_Hot Chestnuts | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 18.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-001-hot-chestnuts-03.webp)

A legend about Saint Martin is associated with this celebration. According to the story, a Roman soldier encountered a freezing poor man during a storm and shared his cloak with him, giving him half. Afterwards, the cold weather gave way to sunshine. This story is linked to the expression _Verão de São Martinho_ — a brief spell of warm weather that sometimes arrives in November.

Chestnuts are not mentioned in the legend itself. Their connection with the celebration comes from a different tradition: the autumn harvest and the custom of gathering to enjoy food together. Thus, a legend about generosity and the custom of gathering around a fire have become associated with the same celebration.

## Kyiv’s Chestnuts

![_Hot Chestnuts | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 18.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-001-hot-chestnuts-04.webp)

And so we return to Kyiv’s chestnuts. The familiar shiny brown fruits known to Ukrainians belong to a different plant — the horse chestnut (_Aesculus hippocastanum_). Despite their resemblance, they are not edible: their seeds contain toxic substances.

At the same time, standardised preparations made from horse chestnut seeds are used in medicine to relieve symptoms of chronic venous insufficiency, including swelling, pain and a feeling of heaviness in the legs. These preparations are made from specially processed extracts, not raw seeds.

In everyday life, horse chestnuts had another use. They were collected in autumn and placed in drawers and wardrobes with clothes, especially woollen and winter garments, in the hope of protecting them from moths. This custom was not limited to Ukraine: references to using horse chestnuts to protect clothing can also be found in European household advice books from the nineteenth century. Today, this can be regarded primarily as an example of a domestic tradition rather than a proven method of moth control.

So, despite the similarity in names and appearance, these are different botanical species with different uses.

## Volunteering

One by one, the chestnuts lost their hard shells and thin skins, becoming ready to eat.

And the older people could relax, taste the warm, peeled chestnuts and enjoy a flavour of the Portuguese autumn that, for many, was familiar from childhood.

Ordinary work that requires a few pairs of hands, a little time and several people willing to take part. Sometimes it is precisely these small tasks that make up the everyday life of a shared space.

## P.S.

No grand events or complicated tasks. Just hot chestnuts and work that is easier and quicker to do together.

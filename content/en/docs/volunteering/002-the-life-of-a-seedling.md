# The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering

![_The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 25.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-002-the-life-of-a-seedling.webp)

**25 November 2026  | VOL'MIG | Pista Mágica | Loja Social de Esposende | G2**

_From the municipal nursery to the green spaces of Esposende._

Can you imagine that around 17,900 plants are propagated every year to green a municipality covering 95.4 km²? Its public spaces already contain 7,666 trees, while the total area of green spaces amounts to approximately 75,500 square metres. Behind these figures lies the continuous work of growing, planting and caring for plants. But before they reach the town's streets, they follow their own journey through the municipal nursery.

## Working with Young Plants

![_The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 25.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-002-the-life-of-a-seedling-01.webp)

Our volunteering session took place at Centro de Educação Ambiental (Environmental Education Centre), located in Marinhas, within the municipality of Esposende.

Our group was assigned the task of removing weeds from young plants. At first, it seemed simple: carefully lift each plant out of its pot together with its root ball, remove the unwanted vegetation and return the seedling to its place.

We worked outdoors, standing around a large table. We placed several trays of potted plants on it. Once cleaned, the seedlings went back into their original pots, and the pots were returned to the trays. One of the most common weeds was couch grass. It proved particularly difficult to remove without pulling out the roots of the seedlings along with it.

The work progressed quickly. Everyone concentrated on their own plants, but we were also constantly chatting. We talked, joked and sometimes played little tricks on one another: someone might quietly place their freshly cleaned seedling in a neighbour's tray. These small jokes did not interrupt the work; they simply made it more enjoyable.

We were working with plants that had already been grown and now needed to be freed from competing vegetation so they could continue developing.

## Rain Changes Our Workplace

![_The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 25.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-002-the-life-of-a-seedling-02.webp)

When it started raining, we moved into the greenhouse. Another group from our team was already working there.

The task was different. There was soil spread across a large table, empty pots, and young plants waiting to be planted.

The work was divided among the participants. Some filled the pots with soil. Others used a small stick to make a hole in the soil and placed a seedling inside. They then pressed the soil around the plant to secure it in its new pot. Someone else collected the plant debris and wheeled the weeds away in a barrow.

Each task was small, but together they formed a sequence of operations to prepare the plants for further growth.

When the rain stopped, we returned to our previous outdoor workplace. There were still plenty of plants waiting to be cleaned.

## Creeping Rosemary

![_The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 25.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-002-the-life-of-a-seedling-03.webp)

Among the plants we worked with, one caught my attention in particular. The label I photographed read _Rosmarinho prostratos_. It referred to creeping rosemary — _Salvia rosmarinus_, Prostratus Group.

During the work, Fátima found a long, spreading plant in one of the pots and exclaimed with delight: “Que linda!” (“How beautiful!”).

Now, looking back at the photographs, I understand her enthusiasm.

Unlike upright forms of rosemary, creeping varieties spread horizontally. Their long stems trail along the ground or cascade over slopes and stone walls. This is an evergreen, aromatic plant with narrow leaves and blue flowers.

But its value goes beyond its decorative qualities.

Creeping rosemary is used as a ground-cover plant. As its stems spread and form a dense cover, the foliage shades the soil surface, limiting the germination of some weeds.

In addition, once established, rosemary tolerates drought well. It prefers sunny locations and well-drained soil. These characteristics make it suitable for landscaping, particularly in coastal areas.

Rosemary can be propagated in several ways, including from cuttings. Vegetative propagation makes it possible to grow new plants from parts of a parent plant while preserving its characteristic features.

And here lies an interesting detail about our work:

> We were removing weeds from pots containing future ground-cover plants — plants that would eventually be able to create a green carpet of their own.

## From the Nursery to Public Spaces

![_The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 25.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-002-the-life-of-a-seedling-04.webp)

During the session, we were told that the plants grown in the municipal nursery are intended for planting in various public spaces: along town streets, on the grounds of schools and hospitals, and at other public institutions.

This changes how we see the work itself. The pot standing in front of you on the table is only a temporary home for the plant. Ahead lies a different environment, where it will continue to grow and play its part in the town's green spaces.

Urban landscaping begins long before a new tree appears on a street or a flower bed takes its finished shape. First there is a seed or a cutting, a young seedling, a pot filled with soil, and the time needed for growth. And there are people who grow, clean, transplant and prepare the plants for the next stage.

## Volunteering

![_The Life of Seedlings | Volunteering. AP | Pivtorak.Studio. 25.11.2026_ © Anna Pivtorak (Kostyuk)](/images/volunteering-002-the-life-of-a-seedling-05.webp)

That day, we did not plant trees along the town's streets or create new flower beds.   

We joined the process much earlier, at the stage when plants are still growing in pots and being prepared for their future locations.

So, behind the emergence of each new green space in Esposende is a whole chain of everyday tasks that often go unnoticed. 

And one of them is the work done around an ordinary table in the municipal nursery.

## P.S.

Each plant still has its own journey ahead — from the nursery to the place where it will become part of the town's landscape. And perhaps, in time, the creeping rosemary will spread so far that individual seedlings will merge into a continuous green carpet.
